# Service Ladder

The service ladder shows how Szymik Digital can move from small, easy-to-buy work into deeper support, automation, and future products.

## 1. Website Fixes / Audits

### Client Problem

The client already has a website, but it is unclear, outdated, slow, hard to update, weak on trust, or not helping local customers take action.

### Offer

A focused audit or improvement sprint covering practical issues such as messaging, structure, calls to action, basic technical hygiene, local relevance, and obvious conversion blockers.

### Outcome

The client gets a clearer view of what is wrong and, where included, quick improvements that make the site more useful.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Sell this when the client is not ready for a rebuild, has a limited budget, or needs an expert review before deciding what to do next.

## 2. Starter Websites

### Client Problem

The client needs a credible online presence but does not yet need a large website or complex functionality.

### Offer

A lean, professional website with clear positioning, key service information, trust signals, and a simple enquiry path.

### Outcome

The business has a credible website that can be shared with prospects, found by local customers, and improved over time.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Sell this to new businesses, small operators, or clients replacing a very basic or weak existing site.

## 3. Local Growth Websites

### Client Problem

The client needs more than a brochure site. They need clearer services, stronger local relevance, and a better foundation for search and enquiries.

### Offer

A more structured website with service pages, location-aware content where appropriate, stronger trust sections, clearer calls to action, and local SEO foundations.

### Outcome

The business has a stronger digital foundation for local visibility, service clarity, and lead generation.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Sell this when the client has clear services, local competition, and a need to be found and trusted by people searching nearby.

## 4. Monthly Support

### Client Problem

The client does not want to maintain the website alone and needs ongoing help with updates, fixes, improvements, and small digital tasks.

### Offer

A monthly support arrangement for website updates, minor improvements, checks, content support, and small technical fixes within agreed limits.

### Outcome

The website stays current, issues are handled faster, and the client has a practical digital partner instead of ad hoc support.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Sell this after a website project, after an audit, or when a client has regular changes but not enough need for a large retainer.

## 5. Automation / Custom Tools

### Client Problem

The client is losing time to repeated admin, manual tracking, disconnected tools, or inconsistent internal processes.

### Offer

A discovery-led automation or custom tool project that identifies the workflow, defines the scope, and builds a practical solution if there is a clear case.

### Outcome

The client gets a simpler workflow, reduced manual effort, better visibility, or a tool that supports a repeated business process.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Sell this when trust already exists, the business problem is specific, and the workflow is repeated enough to justify building a solution.

## 6. Owned Products / SaaS

### Client Problem

Multiple businesses share the same repeated problem, and a reusable product could solve it better than repeated bespoke work.

### Offer

Future-facing owned products built from validated patterns found through client work. CoreShift is part of this long-term product direction.

### Outcome

Szymik Digital can grow beyond services into reusable tools, while still solving practical small-business problems.

### Notes On When To Sell It

Do not sell this as the core early offer. Treat it as future-facing until demand is validated through repeated service work, discovery, and real usage.
