# Client Workflow

## Operating Process

Lead -> Discovery -> Quote -> Deposit -> Build -> Review -> Final Payment -> Launch -> Handover -> Support

The workflow should stay simple, clear, and repeatable. Clients should always know what is needed from them, what is included, what happens next, and when payment is due.

## Discovery Questions

Use these questions to understand fit, scope, and urgency before quoting.

- What does the business do?
- Who are the main customers?
- What services or offers matter most?
- What is not working with the current website or digital setup?
- What outcome would make this project worthwhile?
- Is there an existing website, domain, hosting, or Google Business Profile?
- Which local areas or customer groups matter most?
- What pages, features, or content are needed?
- Who will provide copy, images, logos, logins, and business details?
- Are there examples of websites the client likes or dislikes?
- Are there compliance, privacy, accessibility, or sector-specific requirements?
- What budget range and deadline should be considered?
- Who signs off the work?

## Quote And Scope Principles

- Quote against a defined outcome, not a vague list of tasks
- State exactly what is included
- State what is excluded
- Define page count, revision rounds, content responsibilities, and major functionality
- Separate optional extras from the core project
- Avoid open-ended promises around SEO, rankings, growth, or automation
- For uncertain automation work, quote discovery before quoting a build

## Deposit / Balance Model

Default model:

- Deposit due before work begins
- Balance due before launch, handover, or release of final production access

For larger projects, milestone payments may be used.

Payment terms should be written into the quote before the client approves the work.

## Client Responsibilities

Clients are responsible for providing agreed inputs on time, including:

- Business information
- Service details
- Brand assets
- Images or permission to source suitable assets
- Domain, hosting, website, CMS, analytics, or Google Business Profile access where needed
- Legal policies or approved wording for regulated claims
- Feedback within agreed review windows
- Final approval before launch

If client inputs are late, project timelines may move.

## Revision Rounds

Each quote should define the included review rounds.

Default principle:

- One structured review round after the first complete draft
- One smaller follow-up round for agreed refinements

Revisions should cover changes within the agreed scope. New pages, new features, major direction changes, or repeated rewrites should be treated as scope changes.

## Scope Change Handling

When a request falls outside the agreed scope:

1. Confirm the requested change in writing.
2. Explain whether it affects cost, timeline, or both.
3. Quote the additional work before starting it.
4. Add it to the project record once approved.

Do not absorb unclear extra work silently. Scope clarity protects the client relationship.

## Data / Privacy Handling Basics

- Only request access that is needed for the work
- Use secure access methods where available
- Avoid storing client passwords in plain text
- Remove access that is no longer needed after handover where appropriate
- Do not publish private client information without permission
- Do not invent testimonials, metrics, case study results, or client claims
- Treat client business information, customer data, and internal workflows as confidential

For projects involving personal data, bookings, forms, email tools, analytics, or automations, privacy responsibilities should be identified before launch.

## Handover Checklist

Before handover, confirm:

- Final balance has been paid
- Website has been reviewed and approved
- Key pages and links have been checked
- Contact forms or enquiry paths have been tested
- Mobile layout has been checked
- Domain and hosting setup are confirmed
- Admin access is transferred or confirmed
- Basic editing guidance is provided where relevant
- Any third-party tools are documented
- Ongoing support options are explained

## Support / Maintenance Options

Support can be offered after launch as:

- Ad hoc paid support
- Monthly support for updates and minor improvements
- Website health checks
- Content and page updates
- Local visibility improvements
- Automation or workflow discovery where a repeated problem appears

Support should have clear limits. Monthly support is not a substitute for unlimited redesign, full SEO campaign management, or open-ended custom development unless separately scoped.
