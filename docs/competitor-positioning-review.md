# Competitor Positioning Review And Proof Gap Plan

This review is a practical positioning check for Szymik Digital after the Phase 2 client-flow work and Phase 3 public website improvements.

It is based on the current project docs and public website content. It is not a full external competitor audit and does not make claims about named competitors. The competitor landscape below uses common market categories Ric is likely to meet when selling small-business website work.

## Short Answer

Yes, the website is strong enough to support warm outreach and first client conversations.

Do not delay client conversations for endless polish. The site now explains who Szymik Digital helps, what the main offer pathways are, how enquiries move into review and quote, and why the process should feel calm for small local businesses.

## Current Szymik Digital Positioning

### Main Audience

Szymik Digital is aimed at small, local, service-led businesses that need a clearer digital foundation but do not need a large agency.

Good-fit audiences include:

- Service businesses.
- Trades and home services.
- Care and support providers.
- Clinics, wellness providers, and appointment-led businesses.
- Consultants and professional service providers.
- Sole traders and small teams.
- Local organisations that rely on trust, referrals, and local visibility.

The current location positioning is sensible: based in Todmorden, West Yorkshire, UK-based, and remote-friendly. It gives local credibility without pretending Szymik Digital only serves one town or has a wide agency footprint.

### Offer Pathways

The public website is now organised around four understandable pathways:

- Website Refresh: for existing websites that feel dated, unclear, inconsistent, or hard to use.
- Starter Website: for small businesses that need a credible first website and a simple enquiry route.
- Local Growth Website: for service-led businesses that need clearer service pages, local relevance, trust content, and better enquiry flow.
- Automation / Custom Tool Discovery: for repeated workflow or admin problems that need discovery before any build is promised.

This offer structure is strong because it gives visitors a way to self-identify without forcing them into rigid packages too early.

### Current Trust And Process Strengths

The website now communicates several useful trust points:

- No-pressure enquiry flow.
- Enquiries are reviewed before quoting.
- Clear written quote before paid work starts.
- Agreed scope before build begins.
- Deposit/payment terms confirmed where relevant.
- Preview and feedback stage before launch.
- Agreed changes before final approval.
- Launch checks and handover after approval.
- Friendly communication through the Hannah and Ric workflow.

This is a meaningful advantage for small-business buyers. Many small clients are not just buying a website; they are buying confidence that the process will not become confusing, expensive, or awkward.

### Current Proof Gaps

The proof base is still early. The website is honest about that, which is the right approach.

Current gaps:

- Libra Support Services needs permissioned case-study detail before it can carry more weight.
- There are not yet multiple external client examples.
- No testimonials or reviews are currently published.
- No before/after screenshots are currently available.
- No public outcome metrics are claimed.
- No Google Business Profile review base is visible in the current website content.

These gaps do not block outreach, but they do affect conversion strength with colder prospects.

### Current Enquiry Flow

The enquiry flow is intentionally manual and clear:

1. Visitor reads the public website and chooses the closest pathway, or selects "not sure yet."
2. Visitor submits an enquiry through the static email-draft form or emails directly.
3. Hannah reviews and logs the enquiry.
4. Missing details are requested if needed.
5. Ric reviews fit, scope, technical approach, likely timeline, and quote readiness.
6. Client receives questions, call offer, quote, recommendation, or polite decline.
7. Paid work starts only after written quote approval and payment/deposit terms are met where relevant.

This is a good early-stage setup. It keeps the public site lightweight and avoids overbuilding a CRM before there are enough real enquiries to justify it.

### How The Website Supports The Hannah -> CRM/Ric Workflow

The public copy now supports the internal workflow well:

- Service pages help visitors choose a likely enquiry type.
- Pricing guidance improves budget expectations without promising instant quotes.
- Contact copy asks for current website, desired improvements, timescale, budget range, contact preference, and location/service area.
- The enquiry form sends a visible email draft instead of hiding the process.
- Public process copy prepares clients for review, written quote, preview, feedback, approval, launch, and handover.
- Internal docs give Hannah templates and tracker statuses for triage.
- Internal docs reserve scope, pricing, technical decisions, and launch readiness for Ric.

The website is not pretending to have automation or a client portal. That is good. It matches the current manual process.

## Positioning Recommendation

Use this as the working positioning line:

> Practical websites and digital support for small local businesses, with clear process, friendly communication, and sensible next steps before paid work starts.

This is strong because it is specific enough to be useful, but not so narrow that it blocks early learning.

### What To Emphasise

- Practical websites, not design theatre.
- Small local businesses and service-led teams.
- Website refreshes, starter websites, and local growth websites.
- Discovery-first automation and custom tool support.
- Clear written quotes before paid work starts.
- Scope agreed before build.
- Preview, feedback, approval, launch, and handover.
- Friendly communication and tidy project handling.
- Founder-led technical delivery through Ric.
- Hannah helping keep client communication organised where useful.

### What Not To Emphasise Yet

- Advanced automation as a main lead offer.
- Large agency-style strategy.
- Broad full-service marketing.
- Big SEO promises.
- Guaranteed growth, ranking, or revenue outcomes.
- A large team or mature delivery machine.
- SaaS or owned products as if they are current client offers.

### What Should Wait Until There Is Proof

- Strong public claims about results.
- Detailed SEO ranking improvements.
- Revenue or lead-generation claims.
- Sector dominance claims.
- Formal support package claims if support terms are not yet proven.
- Multiple case studies beyond what is accurate and permissioned.
- Testimonials until real clients have provided them.

### How To Avoid Sounding Bigger Than The Business Is

- Say "Szymik Digital" or "Ric" instead of implying a large team.
- Keep "Hannah helps coordinate" wording natural and occasional.
- Use "we" where it sounds normal, but avoid agency theatre.
- Lead with practical process, not corporate language.
- Be direct about internal/foundation examples.
- Keep concept work clearly labelled.
- Avoid fake proof, fake logos, fake metrics, and vague transformation claims.

## Good Enough To Seek Clients?

Yes.

The site is strong enough for warm outreach and first client conversations now, provided Ric completes a manual browser acceptance pass and fixes any obvious blockers found there.

Do not wait for:

- A perfect portfolio.
- Multiple case studies.
- A polished CRM.
- A full automation system.
- More public pages.
- Endless homepage refinement.

Do fix:

- Obvious mobile readability issues.
- Broken links.
- Typos.
- Confusing CTAs.
- Any wording that accidentally promises instant quotes, guaranteed results, or immediate work starts.

Then improve from real feedback. The next set of refinements should come from real conversations, not more guessing.
